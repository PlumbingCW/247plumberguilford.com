Sewer Camera Inspection in Guilford, CT

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your drain line, so the plumber and you can see the pipe on a screen. It replaces guessing with a picture. We schedule sewer camera inspections in Guilford, CT for repeat clogs, before a repair, and when you are buying a house with an older line.

If sewage is already backing up, call the 24/7 line first. The camera can follow once the line is clear.

What a camera inspection shows

The camera head carries a light and a lens that sends a live image to a monitor. As it moves through the line, the plumber watches for root intrusion, cracks, offset joints, bellies where water collects, scale and grease, foreign objects and collapses. A locator on the surface tracks the camera so the exact position and depth of any problem can be marked.

Most inspections give you a recording and a clear idea of what, if anything, needs doing. Some also give a rough length of the line and the number of bends. The point is to decide between cleaning and repair on evidence.

When a camera inspection is worth it

These are the reasons homeowners and buyers ask for one.

  • ✓A drain that keeps clogging. Repeating clogs mean something in the line is catching debris.
  • ✓Before you buy an older house. A line in poor shape can mean a large expense soon after closing.
  • ✓Before hydro jetting an old line. To make sure the pipe will survive the pressure. See hydro jetting.
  • ✓After a repair. To confirm the new section is clear and aligned.
  • ✓When tree roots are near the line. To see whether any have entered.
  • ✓When a backup cannot be explained. The camera tells whether the blockage is in your line or beyond.

Inspections for house buyers

In a house built decades ago, the sewer line is one of the largest hidden costs. A standard home inspection does not usually include a camera look at the lateral. A buyer can ask for one, and the findings can be used to negotiate or to plan for a repair.

Guilford has a historic town center, shoreline neighborhoods and newer subdivisions, so the pipe materials and ages vary widely from street to street. Because the town has no municipal sewer, a house you buy here has an on-site system, so ask for the septic inspection as well. The camera only covers the line, not the tank.

The camera and your septic system

The camera can inspect the line from the house to the tank, but not the tank or the leach field. In Guilford the health department reviews and inspects new, repaired and enlarged septic systems, and its page says several areas of town must be inspected yearly. Tell the dispatcher when the tank was last pumped, and keep the pumper's record with the camera video.

What affects the price of a camera inspection

A clear price comes before the visit. These details set it.

  • Length of the line.A short run costs less than a long one.
  • Access.A cleanout makes the camera easy to insert. Without one, it goes in through a toilet or a vent.
  • Locating.Surface locating adds time but is worth it before any dig.
  • Cleaning needed first.A blocked line has to be cleared before the camera can pass.
  • Recording and report.You can ask for the video and notes.

A camera inspection to investigate a clog is usually a plumbing expense, not an insurance one. After a covered loss, ask your insurer whether the inspection is part of the claim.

Need to see what is in your line?

Call the line and describe what is happening. If water is rising now, say so, and the call becomes an emergency. See sewer line repair for what comes after the camera.
