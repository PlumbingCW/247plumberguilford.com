Boiler Repair in Guilford, CT

Many houses along the Connecticut shoreline are heated by a hot water boiler that sends water through baseboard or radiators. When it stops, the house cools quickly, and in a hard freeze the pipes are at risk too. We help with boiler repair in Guilford, CT, from a bleed that restores heat to a boiler that has stopped firing.

If the house has no heat in freezing weather, call the 24/7 line and say so. That is the situation where a pipe freeze becomes a burst pipe.

No heat: what to check before you call

These checks are safe for homeowners and give the dispatcher useful information.

  • 1Look at the thermostat. Confirm it is set to heat, with a temperature above the room reading, and that its batteries are good.
  • 2Check the boiler switch and the breaker. Boilers have an emergency switch near the stairs, often mistaken for a light switch.
  • 3Check the fuel. For an oil boiler, see whether the tank gauge shows fuel. For a gas or propane boiler, check that the supply valve is open.
  • 4Read the pressure gauge. A hot water boiler is typically around 12 to 15 pounds when cold, and the reading rises as the water heats. Tell the dispatcher what yours reads.
  • 5Look for water around the boiler. A puddle moves the problem from comfort to damage.
  • 6Do not press the oil burner reset button more than once. Repeated resets can flood the combustion chamber with unburned fuel.

Oil, gas and propane: three fuels, three sets of rules

Heating fuel on the shoreline is mixed, and neighboring towns such as North Branford lean heavily on oil. The state's utility notes say that natural gas service in Guilford varies by street and that many homes use oil or propane. In neighboring Madison and Clinton, Census data show that more than half of occupied homes heat with fuel oil. For a homeowner, that means the boiler problem is often at the burner: nozzle, filter, ignition or the oil supply.

Gas piping and appliances in Connecticut follow NFPA 54 and NFPA 58 through the Fire Safety Code. Gas leaks and propane leaks are emergencies that come before the repair. Get everyone outside, then call 911 and Southern Connecticut Gas from there. The state lists its emergency line as 800-513-8898.

Licenses and permits for boiler work in Connecticut

Connecticut licenses heating work separately from plumbing. The Department of Consumer Protection lists heating, piping and cooling classes in the S series, with S-3 and S-4 covering the installation, repair, replacement and maintenance of piping, appliances and accessories for heating systems and boilers, along with oil and gas piping. Burner work has its own B classes, and gas piping and appliances have G classes. Ask whoever you hire which class they hold and check it on the state license lookup.

The Guilford Building Department lists boilers and HVAC as mechanical work, with its own permit type. It also lists removing an indoor oil tank as work that needs a plumbing sub-permit. Ask the contractor which permits your job needs and who files them.

Baseboard heat, bleeding and the water side

Air collects in hot water heating lines, and it blocks the flow of hot water to the farthest baseboards. Bleeding releases that air through small valves, and a boiler that refills with fresh water takes in more air. If one zone keeps losing heat, the fix may be a purging pass, an air separator or a circulator.

A boiler that feeds an indirect water heater supplies hot water too. When the boiler goes down in winter, the hot water goes with it. Our water heater repair page explains how the two systems relate.

Keeping pipes safe when the heat is out

If the boiler cannot be restored quickly in freezing weather, the priority is to keep the water in the pipes from freezing. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls, let a faucet drip, and consider draining pipes in unheated areas.

If you do find a frozen or split pipe, see burst pipe repair. The state code uses a frost depth of 42 inches, and the January average low at the coastal New Haven Tweed station is about 23 degrees Fahrenheit per NOAA, which means a hard freeze is not rare.

What affects the price of boiler repair

Repair prices depend on the part and the fuel.

  • The part.A thermostat or zone valve is cheaper than a circulator, and a circulator is cheaper than a control or boiler section.
  • The fuel and burner.Oil burners, gas valves and propane equipment differ.
  • Age.An old boiler may cost more to repair than its remaining life justifies.
  • Access.A boiler in a tight utility room takes longer.
  • The hour.No-heat calls on nights and weekends are quoted as emergencies.

Insurance usually covers sudden water damage from a failed boiler or a freeze burst, and not the worn-out boiler itself. Photograph everything before cleanup.
