Smelly Drains in Guilford, CT: Causes and Fixes

A drain that smells usually has one of four causes: a dry trap that no longer blocks sewer gas, a film of grease and soap inside the pipe, a blocked or missing vent, or a problem further along the line. In Guilford that last one is often a full septic tank. The odor tells you something about which. Rotten eggs point to sewer gas, a musty smell to buildup, and a sharp sour smell to food waste.

Start with the cheapest checks: run water in every fixture for a minute, clean the strainer, and smell each drain separately. If only one drain smells, the cause is local to that fixture. If every drain smells, look at the system as a whole, and in Guilford that means the septic tank.

Dry traps and the water barrier

Every fixture has a P-trap, a bend that holds a plug of water to block sewer gas. If a basement floor drain, a guest bath sink or a laundry standpipe is not used for weeks, the water in the trap evaporates and gas rises through the pipe. The fix is to run water until the trap fills.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Connecticut adopts requires fixtures to be trapped for this reason. A spoonful of cooking oil on top slows evaporation in a drain you rarely use.

Traps can also be siphoned dry when a vent is blocked, which is why a smell that comes and goes with weather or heavy water use deserves a vent check.

Guilford note: septic systems and the odor question

Guilford has no municipal sewer. The health department says sewage disposal is handled on-site by septic systems and that every one must be pumped every five years, with several areas inspected yearly based on lot size and soil. If every drain in your house smells and you cannot remember the last pump-out, the tank is the first thing to check.

The health department's form asks pumpers to return a record of each cleaning within 30 days, so the department should have a record of your last one. Ask when you call. Septic repairs need a health department permit, and the town advises using licensed septic contractors. The department's office is at 50 Boston Street and its phone is (203) 453-8118.

Buildup inside the pipe

Grease, soap, food particles and hair coat the inside of drain pipes, and bacteria feed on that film. The result is a sour or musty smell that returns days after you rinse the sink. Pulling the trap and scrubbing it often solves a single fixture. If the smell is stronger after you pour hot water, the buildup runs further down the line, and our drain cleaning service or hydro jetting clears the coating.

Garbage disposals add their own smell from food stuck under the splash guard. Clean the rubber baffle with a brush and dish soap.

When to call about a drain smell, and what not to do

These situations need a call, not a home remedy.

  • ✓A strong sewage smell in the house that does not fade. Gas from a failed seal, a cracked drain pipe or a full tank.
  • ✓A smell with slow drains in more than one fixture. The main line or the tank is the suspect. See sewer line cleaning.
  • ✓Wet or green patches over the septic area in the yard. This points to a system problem. Call the health department and a septic professional.
  • ✓A smell of natural gas. Leave, call 911 and the gas utility from outside.
  • ✓A smell after a flood or heavy rain. Water may be entering the system through a failed seal or cleanout.

Vents and the roof

A vent pipe leaves the plumbing system through the roof and lets air in so water can flow. When a vent clogs, drains gurgle, traps empty and odors enter the house. A plumber can clear a blocked vent with a cable from the roof. If a vent has no cover or is too close to a window, smells can drift into the house even when the system works.

New bathrooms or kitchen remodels sometimes leave a fixture without a proper vent. If the smell started after work was done, mention it on the call.

A simple weekly routine that stops most drain odors

Most drain smells are a maintenance problem that you can head off in a few minutes. Rinse sink strainers weekly. Pour a kettle of hot water down kitchen drains after greasy dishes. Run water in guest bathrooms, basement floor drains and laundry standpipes that rarely get used.

Wipe the underside of the sink stopper and pull it out for cleaning every few months. Hair and soap collect on the stopper arm, and that film is the source of many bathroom sink odors. Clean the overflow channel with a thin brush, because the opening is a hidden place for mold.

If you do all of that and the smell stays, treat it as a signal that something deeper is happening, not as a cleaning failure.
