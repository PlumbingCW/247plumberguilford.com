Baseboard Heat Not Working: Bleed It or Call a Plumber?

If part of a hot water baseboard system is cold while the boiler runs, trapped air is the most likely cause, and bleeding the system often fixes it. If the whole house is cold, check the thermostat, the boiler switch, the fuel supply and the pressure gauge before you call. If you see water around the boiler, or the boiler will not fire, stop and call.

This guide covers how a hot water system works, the safe steps a homeowner can take, and the signs that a call is better than a wrench. It ends with notes on heating fuel, licenses and permits in Guilford.

How a hot water baseboard system works

A boiler heats water. A circulator pump pushes it through copper pipe to finned-tube baseboards in each room. As the water gives up heat, it returns to the boiler. Zone valves or separate circulators divide the house so each thermostat controls its own area. An expansion tank takes up the water's growth as it heats, and a relief valve protects the system from over-pressure.

The system is closed. It should not lose water, and air should not need to be added. When a zone goes cold, either air has collected or something has stopped moving the water.

Before you bleed anything, check the basics

These checks take a few minutes.

  • 1Thermostat. Set to heat, above room temperature, with working batteries.
  • 2Boiler switch and breaker. The emergency switch is often near the top of the cellar stairs.
  • 3Fuel. Oil level, gas valve open, or propane supply in the tank.
  • 4Pressure gauge. A cold system is typically around 12 to 15 pounds. Near zero means water has been lost.
  • 5Circulator pump. Is it warm and humming? A silent pump may be stuck or off.

Guilford note: fuels, licenses and permits for heating work

The state's gas safety page lists Southern Connecticut Gas as the gas utility for Guilford and notes that coverage varies by street, with many homes using oil or propane. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and the gas utility from outside, and the state lists SCG's emergency number as 800-513-8898.

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbing and heating separately. Its heating, piping and cooling classes in the S series cover boilers, and burner and gas work have their own classes. The Guilford Building Department lists boilers and HVAC as mechanical work with its own permit type, requires a valid state license and liability insurance, and takes applications online.

How to bleed a baseboard zone

Bleeding releases trapped air through a small valve at a high point in the zone, usually on the end of the baseboard or at a bleeder near the boiler. Turn the boiler to cool and the circulator off, if you can. Put a small bucket or cloth under the valve and open it slowly with a key or a flathead screwdriver. Air hisses out first, then water. Close the valve when a steady stream of water appears.

Check the pressure gauge. If you have released water, it may have dropped. Many systems refill on their own through a fill valve. If pressure does not recover, or the gauge climbs too high, stop and call. Never open the relief valve to bleed air.

When no heat becomes a plumbing emergency

In freezing weather, a house without heat is at risk of frozen pipes within hours if the temperature outside stays low and the house is not insulated well. The Connecticut State Building Code uses a frost depth of 42 inches, and the January average low in coastal New Haven County is about 23 degrees Fahrenheit according to NOAA. Open cabinet doors, let a faucet drip and call. A burst pipe is far more costly than a service call.

Maintenance that prevents a cold house

Have the boiler serviced before the heating season. Many manufacturers recommend yearly service, and the technician cleans the burner, checks the controls and tests safety devices. Check the pressure gauge in the fall. Make sure the baseboards are free of dust and that furniture and curtains do not block them.

Know where your boiler switch, your fuel shutoff and your main water valve are. Keep the number of a heating professional you trust near the boiler. For oil, check the tank level monthly in winter, since a tank that runs dry after the last delivery needs to be bled before the burner will fire. For propane, the same applies to the tank gauge.

Warning signs from a boiler

These are the early signs that a service call is overdue.

  • ✓Yellow flame or soot around the boiler. This points to poor combustion and needs a professional.
  • ✓Rust or drips at joints and valves. A slow leak that will get worse.
  • ✓Frequent cycling on and off. A control or sizing problem.
  • ✓A new smell of fuel oil or exhaust. Call and ventilate the area.
  • ✓A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds. Leave and call 911.
