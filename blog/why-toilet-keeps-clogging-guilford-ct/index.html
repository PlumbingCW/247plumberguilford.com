Why Your Toilet Keeps Clogging in Guilford, CT

A toilet that clogs once is a nuisance. One that clogs every few days has a reason. The usual causes are too much paper or flushed wipes, a low-flow design that needs a decent flush to push waste through, mineral buildup in the jets, a partial blockage past the trap or a drain and vent problem further along. Which one you have decides the fix.

This guide helps you work through those causes in order, from the cheapest to the most involved. It ends with the signs that a repeating clog is not the toilet at all but the line behind it.

Work through the causes in order

Each step rules something out. Stop when the clogs stop.

  • 1Look at what you flush. Only toilet paper and human waste. Wipes, even those labeled flushable, paper towels and cotton swabs do not break down.
  • 2Check how the toilet flushes. Watch the bowl. A weak, slow swirl means the jets under the rim are partly blocked by mineral deposits.
  • 3Try a flange plunger. It has a flap that fits into the drain opening. Plunge with steady pressure, not wild pumping.
  • 4Use a closet auger. A flexible tool made for toilets that reaches past the trap and does not scratch the porcelain.
  • 5Test the other drains. If the tub or sink slows when you flush, the problem is the line, not the toilet.

How toilets are designed to clear waste

A toilet works by siphon. The tank empties into the bowl fast enough to fill the trapway and pull waste along with it. A toilet that is slow to refill, has a low water level in the tank or flushes too weakly will not build the siphon. The fix may be adjusting the fill valve, cleaning the jets or replacing a worn flapper.

Older toilets that use more water per flush clear waste with brute force. Newer efficient models depend on good design and a clear trapway. Neither will work if the drain behind them is partly blocked.

Guilford note: what a septic tank does with wipes and paper

Guilford has no municipal sewer. The town's plan of conservation and development says so, and the health department says waste goes to on-site septic systems. In a septic tank, solids settle and liquids move on to a leach field. Wipes and heavy paper settle slowly or not at all and can pass into the outlet or the field, which is why plumbers and pumpers ask residents to keep them out.

The health department requires pump-outs every five years and asks pumpers to return a cleaning record within 30 days. If a toilet clogs and you cannot say when the tank was last pumped, find the record or call the department at (203) 453-8118. The department says septic repairs need its permit and points owners to licensed septic contractors.

Mineral buildup and rim jets

The small holes under the rim of a bowl release water that rinses and helps push waste. Minerals can clog these holes over the years, which weakens the flush. Pour a cup of white vinegar into the overflow tube and let it sit before flushing. For stubborn deposits, a small mirror and a wire help you see and clear the jets.

If you have hard water, the problem returns. Ask your water provider for a hardness estimate. Connecticut Water, which serves Guilford, says its customer service can estimate hardness for your area even though its reports do not publish it.

When the toilet is the symptom

A toilet is the highest-volume fixture on the drain line, so it often shows a line problem first. Roots that enter old pipe, a section that sags or a cleanout cap that has failed all produce repeating clogs. A camera inspection finds the cause. Our toilet repair service and sewer camera inspection cover the two sides of this.

If sewage backs up into a tub or floor drain when you flush, stop using water and call at once.

Using a plunger and an auger properly

A flange plunger has a rubber flap that fits into the drain opening. Cover the flap with water, press down slowly to push air out, then pull up sharply. The pull is what moves the clog. Repeat several times before you give up. Keep the bathroom door closed in case water splashes.

A closet auger is a short flexible cable inside a rigid tube with a curved end for the toilet bowl. Feed it into the drain, crank the handle and push gently. When you feel resistance, keep turning to break it up or hook it. Withdraw slowly and flush to test. If the auger will not pass, the blockage is farther than a closet auger reaches, which is the point to call a plumber.
