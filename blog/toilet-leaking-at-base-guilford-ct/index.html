Toilet Leaking at the Base in Guilford, CT

Water around the base of a toilet almost always comes from one of three places: the seal under the bowl, the supply line and shutoff valve, or condensation on the tank. A wax ring or bad seal is the cause more often than the other two combined. Dry the floor, flush once and watch where the first new drop appears.

If it is the seal, the toilet has to come up, the flange gets checked and a new ring or seal goes in. That is a one-visit repair when the floor is sound. This guide explains how to tell the causes apart, what is safe to try yourself and when a leak has gone far enough that you should call.

Find out where the water really comes from

Before you blame the seal, rule out the simpler causes. Wipe the floor, tank, supply line and bowl completely dry with a towel. Then check in this order, watching for the first bead of water.

  • Tank sweat. On humid days a cold tank drips onto the bowl and floor. The water is clean and appears on the outside of the tank evenly, not at one point.
  • Supply line and valve. Run a dry tissue around the valve at the wall and along the braided line. A wet tissue means a leaking fitting.
  • Tank bolts and tank-to-bowl gasket. Look at the underside of the tank where it meets the bowl. Drips there run down the back of the bowl to the floor.
  • The base. If water appears only after you flush, and at the bottom edge of the bowl, the seal is the suspect.

Guilford note: septic systems and permit rules for toilet work

Guilford has no municipal sewer, according to the town's plan of conservation and development, and the health department says sewage disposal is handled on-site by septic systems. A toilet that leaks at the base sends water to the floor, not to the tank, but a leaking seal can also let sewer gas into the house. A house on a septic system also has a tank that must be pumped every five years.

On permits, the state code lists resetting a toilet among work that does not need one, provided the pipes, valves and fixtures are not replaced or moved. If your repair turns up a failed drain pipe under the floor, replacing concealed drain pipe counts as new work under the same code section and needs a permit. The Guilford Building Department takes permit applications online and asks for a valid state license and liability insurance.

Why the seal fails

A wax ring sits between the bottom of the toilet and the closet flange, a ring screwed to the floor over the drain. It compresses to keep water and sewer gas from leaking out. It fails when the toilet rocks and breaks the seal, when the flange is set too low or cracked, or when the ring simply ages and hardens.

The first sign is often a faint sewer smell in the bathroom, before any water shows. A toilet that has leaked slowly for months can soften the subfloor around it. Press on the floor beside the bowl. If it feels spongy, the floor is already damaged and the repair is bigger than a ring.

Should you reset it yourself?

A handy homeowner can reset a toilet. It means shutting off the water, draining the tank and bowl, disconnecting the supply, removing the bolts and lifting a heavy toilet straight up. Wear gloves. Cover the open drain with a rag, since sewer gas rises and a dropped bolt can fall in.

Scrape off the old wax, check the flange for cracks, and set the new ring or a wax-free seal before lowering the bowl straight down and tightening the bolts evenly. Overtightening cracks the porcelain. Many people find the job easier than expected and the lifting harder.

Call a plumber when the flange is cracked or below the floor level, or when the floor feels soft. Call when the toilet is wall-hung or on a second floor above a finished ceiling, or when a leak might have reached the ceiling below. If the toilet is leaking right now

These steps limit the damage until a repair is made.

Close the supply valve

Turn the oval handle behind the toilet clockwise. If it will not turn, shut the main.

Stop using the toilet

Every flush adds water to the floor.

Mop and dry

Standing water soaks into subfloor and tile grout.

Check the ceiling below

A stain there means the leak has traveled and needs prompt attention.
