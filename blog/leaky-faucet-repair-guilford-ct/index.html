Leaking Faucet Repair in Guilford, CT

A dripping faucet usually needs one small part: a washer, an O-ring, a cartridge or a valve seat. Which one depends on the faucet design, and you can find out by looking at how many handles it has and where the water leaks. Shut off the supply valves under the sink before you take anything apart.

This guide walks through the four common faucet designs, shows where each one leaks, and explains when replacing the faucet is cheaper than repairing it. It also covers what is different about faucets on water from Guilford's public system and on private wells.

Four faucet designs and where each one leaks

Look at your faucet. How many handles it has and how they move tell you the design.

DesignHow to recognize itUsual leakPart to replace
CompressionTwo handles that screw downDrips from the spout when closedRubber washer and seat
CartridgeSingle or double handle that lifts or turns smoothlyDrips from the spout or leaks at the handleCartridge and O-rings
BallSingle handle over a rounded capLeaks at the base of the handleSeals and springs, ball if worn
Ceramic discSingle handle, quarter-turn feelDrips from the spoutDisc cartridge

Step one: stop the water, then find the leak

Close the two shutoff valves under the sink, hot and cold, then open the faucet to drain it. Plug the sink drain so a dropped screw does not vanish. Take a photograph at every step of disassembly. Photos make reassembly easy and show the part you need to match.

A drip from the spout means the part that closes the water path is worn. A leak around the handle means the packing or O-ring has failed. Water pooling under the sink may be a supply line or the base gasket and not the faucet at all. Dry everything and watch for the first drop.

Guilford note: public water, wells and what a leak repair needs

Public water in Guilford comes from Connecticut Water's Guilford System, which draws on surface reservoirs and groundwater wells. The company says its annual water quality reports do not include hardness, but that customer service can give an estimate for your part of the system at 1-800-286-5700. If your faucet aerator clogs with white flakes, that is the number to ask for.

Some Guilford shoreline neighborhoods were on individual wells when the town studied them in 2011, and well water can carry sediment and minerals that wear faucet parts faster. If you are on a well, a sediment filter before the faucet can cut down on repairs. On permits, simple faucet repairs fall under the leak-repair exemption in the state code.

When repair is not worth it

A replacement faucet is often the better choice in these cases.

  • ✓The faucet body is corroded or pitted. New parts will not seal against a damaged seat.
  • ✓The finish is flaking or the spout wobbles. The faucet is at the end of its life.
  • ✓The parts are no longer made. Discontinued models can cost more to repair than to replace.
  • ✓The leak keeps coming back. A seat that is rough will chew through new washers.
  • ✓You are remodeling. A new faucet costs little compared with the sink and counter.

Supply lines and shutoff valves: the hidden leakers

Many leaks blamed on faucets are actually from the braided supply lines or the small stop valves under the sink. If a valve drips at the stem or will not turn, replace it before it fails during the repair. Old braided lines with corroded fittings can burst without warning. For handling those, our faucet and valve repair page explains the repair side.

A faucet that has been leaking for a long time can leave a stained, swollen cabinet floor. Check for soft particleboard, which swells when wet, and replace it if it crumbles.

A cartridge swap, step by step

This is the most common faucet repair. It takes about half an hour once the water is off and you have the right cartridge.

  • 1Shut off the water and open the faucet. Close both stops under the sink, then open the faucet to let the pressure out.
  • 2Remove the handle. Pry off the cap, undo the set screw and lift the handle away.
  • 3Unscrew the retaining nut or clip. Photograph how it sits before you remove it.
  • 4Pull the cartridge straight up. Twist it gently if it sticks. Note which way the stem faces.
  • 5Match the new cartridge to the old one. Bring the old cartridge to the store, since small differences matter.
  • 6Reassemble in reverse and test. Turn the water on slowly and check for leaks at the handle and spout.

Low flow and sputtering are not always the cartridge

A faucet that sputters or runs weakly often has a clogged aerator, the small screen at the tip of the spout. Unscrew it, soak it in vinegar and rinse out the flakes. If the whole house is weak, the cause is upstream, such as a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator or a leak, and our water service line page explains where to look.

If hot water is weak but cold is fine, the problem may be the heater or its valve, not the faucet. A mixing valve that no longer balances temperature can also make the shower scalding or cold in turns, which is a safety issue and a reason to call.

When to stop and call a plumber

Call if the shutoff valves under the sink will not close, if the faucet is a wall-mounted or tub-and-shower model with parts behind the wall, or if you see water stains on the ceiling below. Call if the faucet body is corroded or the leak is at the base and you cannot reach the connections.

A plumber also brings the right part the first time. Faucet parts are specific to the brand and model, so a photo of the faucet and any name stamped on it helps. If the leak is part of a bigger problem, such as several fixtures dripping because of high pressure, say so on the call.
