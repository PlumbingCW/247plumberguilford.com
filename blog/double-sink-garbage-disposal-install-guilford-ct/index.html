Installing a Garbage Disposal in a Double Sink

A garbage disposal in a double sink connects to one basin and shares a drain with the other through a tee and a continuous waste pipe. The job needs a disposal, a mounting kit, a power source with a switch, a dishwasher connection if you have one and a drain that slopes properly to the trap. It is a project a confident homeowner can do and a plumber does faster.

This guide explains how the drain is laid out, what to buy, the order of the work and the points where installations go wrong. It also covers an issue specific to Guilford: nearly every house uses a septic system, and a disposal adds solids that the tank has to handle.

How a double sink drain is laid out

Each basin has a strainer and a tailpiece. Without a disposal, the two tailpieces join at a tee, and a single trap serves both. With a disposal, the disposal mounts under one basin and its outlet connects to the tee. The other basin drains through a tailpiece that joins the same tee. The trap arm then leaves toward the wall.

The disposal has a side port for the dishwasher drain. A plastic knockout plug blocks that port until you open it. If it is not removed, the dishwasher cannot drain, a mistake that sends dishwater back into the machine.

Guilford note: disposals, septic tanks and who is licensed

Every Guilford house has an on-site septic system, because the town has no municipal sewer. A disposal grinds food into small particles that add to the solids settling in the tank. The health department's five-year pump-out requirement applies whether or not you use one, and Connecticut Water's water quality report advises inspecting septic tanks every two years.

On licensing, the state's Department of Consumer Protection says people doing electrical, HVAC, plumbing, heating and similar jobs must have the appropriate Connecticut license. The Guilford Building Department asks for a valid state license and liability insurance when a contractor applies for a permit, and it handles plumbing and electrical permits as separate types.

The order of the work

This sequence keeps the sink usable as long as possible.

  • 1Turn off power and water. Switch off the circuit and close the stops under the sink.
  • 2Remove the old drain parts. Take off the trap, tailpiece and old strainer.
  • 3Mount the sink flange. Seat it on a rope of putty and tighten the mounting ring.
  • 4Hang the disposal. Lift it onto the mount and lock it into place.
  • 5Connect the discharge tube and the second basin. Adjust the pipes so they slope down to the tee.
  • 6Attach the dishwasher hose. Knock out the plug and clamp the hose, looping it high.
  • 7Wire it and test. Restore power, run water and check every joint for drips.

Where installations go wrong

The common errors are a missing slope in the drain, a dishwasher plug left in place, a flange that was not sealed and a power connection made without a switch. A disposal that leaks at the top has a failed flange seal, one that leaks at the side has failed internally. For repair of a unit that is already installed, see garbage disposal repair.

If the second basin fills when the disposal runs, the line beyond the tee is partly blocked. That is a drain cleaning job, not a disposal problem.

Who is allowed to do the electrical part

Connecticut says people doing electrical, plumbing, heating and similar work must hold the appropriate Connecticut license. A homeowner doing work in their own house is a separate question that the state statute addresses, and this guide does not state what it permits. If the electrical connection is not simple, ask a licensed professional.

A plumber who installs a disposal either does the wiring where the license allows or brings in an electrician. Ask which, and who is responsible for the permit if one is needed.

Choosing a disposal: motor size and features

Disposals are rated by horsepower, and the usual range for a home is one-third to one horsepower. A larger motor grinds tougher waste, runs more quietly and jams less often, but it costs more. A household that cooks often and scrapes plates into the sink benefits from a stronger motor. A two-person home with light use gets by with a smaller unit.

Look at the grinding stages, the sound insulation and the warranty. Continuous feed models run while you hold a switch and batch feed models run only with the stopper in place. A batch feed model is a safety choice for homes with children, since it cannot run with a hand in the drain.

Maintenance that keeps a disposal working

A few habits extend its life and prevent clogs.

  • ✓Run cold water while it runs and for 15 seconds after. Cold water keeps grease solid so it is chopped and flushed.
  • ✓Feed it small amounts. A pile of scraps jams more easily than a trickle.
  • ✓Keep fibrous and starchy foods out. Celery strings, potato peels and rice swell and clump.
  • ✓Never pour grease into it. Grease coats the pipe beyond the disposal.
  • ✓Clean the baffle and flush with ice and citrus peel sometimes. Dislodges residue and freshens it.
