Water Heater Installation in Guilford, CT

A water heater installation in Guilford, CT is more than swapping one tank for another. The fuel, the vent, the relief valve, the expansion tank and the drain line all have to match the new unit and the home it sits in. We install tank and tankless water heaters and plan the job before the old unit comes out.

If your heater has already failed, start with emergency water heater replacement. This page is for planned installations, new construction and upgrades.

What a proper installation includes

A water heater is a pressure vessel that holds a lot of stored energy. Installing one correctly means a temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe termination, a drain pan where a leak would damage finished space, a shutoff valve on the cold inlet and a vent that carries combustion gases out of the house.

On a gas or oil unit, the vent diameter, the pitch of horizontal runs and the clearance to combustibles all matter. A vent that slopes the wrong way or leaks combustion products is a carbon monoxide risk. We also ask about combustion air, because a tight basement or closet can starve a burner.

The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers relief valve discharge piping, and the manufacturer's manual adds its own requirements.

Where municipal or well pressure runs high, a thermal expansion tank absorbs the volume increase as water heats. Without one, repeated pressure spikes can weep the relief valve or shorten the heater's life.

Which type of water heater fits your home

Each type has strengths. The right one depends on fuel, space, household size and budget.

TypeGood fit whenWatch for
Standard tank, gas or oilExisting fuel and flue are in good conditionVenting condition, tank age, standby heat loss

Heat pump models may qualify for rebates that change from year to year. Ask your electric provider and the state energy programs before you buy.

Questions we ask before we quote

These details let us bring the right parts the first time.

  • ✓What fuel does the current heater use? Oil, propane, natural gas and electric each need different installation parts.
  • ✓Where is it, and how is it vented? Basement, closet, garage or utility room, and the path of the flue.
  • ✓How many people and bathrooms? Peak demand decides tank size or tankless capacity.
  • ✓How old is the plumbing around it? Old valves, corroded nipples and seized unions turn a simple swap into a longer job.
  • ✓Is there a floor drain or drain pan? A leak should have somewhere to go besides your floor.

Installations on the Connecticut shoreline

Guilford sits on Long Island Sound, and a heater in a low basement, closet or crawlspace on the shoreline can sit in flood-prone ground. Raising it off the floor can be part of a good plan. We do not guess at flood zone status, so look up your property on the FEMA flood map, and ask the town floodplain manager what rules apply to your address.

Homes with oil heat often tie the water heater to the boiler through an indirect tank. If you are replacing a boiler as well, plan both together. Our boiler repair page covers problems on that side.

What changes the cost of a water heater installation

Replacing a heater is not one price for every house, so the quote follows these items and is clear before work starts.

  • Unit type and size.Capacity and efficiency drive the equipment cost.
  • Venting work.Reusing a sound flue is cheaper than building a new one.
  • Code upgrades.Expansion tanks, drain pans, and a proper relief valve line are required for safe operation.
  • Access.A heater in a tight attic or tall basement stair costs more to move.
  • Permits.Permit rules vary, so we ask the Guilford building department what applies to your project.

Replacement because of a sudden failure is the focus of our emergency replacement service. Installation as a planned upgrade is often not covered by insurance. For a real number, call the 24/7 line and say whether you want a quote or an appointment.

After the install

We test the pressure, check for leaks, confirm that gas appliances light and vent properly and show you where the shutoff valves and the relief valve are. Keep the manual and the model and serial numbers. If you use our tankless water heater service later for flushing, those records help.
