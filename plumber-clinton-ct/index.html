Clinton's flood history, in the town's own words

The town's April 2022 coastal community resilience report says Hurricane Sandy impacted 54 properties through storm surge and Tropical Storm Irene impacted 38, all on Long Island Sound or its rivers. Most properties had about a foot of flooding, and the deepest had over four feet. The town's plan of conservation and development names Pratt Road near the Hammonasset River and the southern ends of Commerce and Grove Streets near the Town Dock as areas that flooded during Sandy.

Beach Park Road and the causeway sit at about eight feet above sea level and flood even in lesser rain events, which can cut off Kelsey Point and Clinton Beach. For a plumber, those facts matter in two ways. A basement or crawlspace in these areas may need a sump pump and a check valve, and a water heater or boiler on the floor of a flood-prone basement is a candidate for raising.

Emergency dispatch for Clinton is available around the clock, including nights when flooding limits access to a beach neighborhood.

No public sewer, and the town enforces septic care

The town's own resilience report says there is currently no sewer system in Clinton and properties rely on individual septic systems. The Water Pollution Control Commission acts as the town's WPCA and oversees the on-site wastewater management ordinance, which requires septic tanks to be pumped at least every five years, seasonal properties included. The town has been under a state consent order since 1997 to address groundwater pollution from septic systems.

When a Clinton drain backs up, ask whether the tank is due for pumping before anything else. Our drain cleaning service and sewer line cleaning service explain how a plumber sorts a house-side clog from a tank problem. The Connecticut River Area Health District handles environmental health for the town.

Water supply: public water in the south, wells elsewhere

Connecticut Water, which is headquartered in Clinton, supplies the town's public water. The town's 2015 plan says about 45 percent of residential properties, mostly south of the Interstate 95 corridor, are served by public water. The town's Rocky Ledge water main project was prompted by well tests that showed nitrates, coliform bacteria and low pH in many wells near septic systems.

If your well water looks or smells different, tell the plumber. A plumber is not a lab, but a pressure tank, a filter or a water treatment unit may point to a problem that needs testing through the health district.

Permits in Clinton: separate mechanical permits

The Clinton Building Department issues permits for new and renovated buildings, electrical, plumbing, heating, air conditioning, demolitions and gas and oil tank work. Since January 1, 2025, the town says all projects require separate mechanical permits for electrical, plumbing and heating. Every application needs a certificate of insurance, a workers compensation affidavit and the contractor's license.

In practice, a replacement water heater or boiler in Clinton can involve more than one permit. Ask your plumber which apply, and who files.

Clinton and the towns beside it

Clinton's neighbors are Madison to the west, Westbrook to the east and Killingworth to the north. Westbrook is a mention-only area for our line, and calls from there are taken the same way. Interstate 95 Exit 63 serves Route 81 and Clinton Crossing Premium Outlets, and the town has a Shore Line East station.

Clinton Village and High Street are the town's two National Register historic districts, and Liberty Green has a local historic district. The older houses around them are where a plumber finds the oldest pipe in town.
