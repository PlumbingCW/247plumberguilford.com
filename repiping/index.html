Repiping in Guilford, CT

Repiping replaces the supply pipes inside your house, the ones that carry water from the entry point to every faucet, fixture and appliance. In Guilford, CT, it is usually considered when old galvanized steel is clogging with rust, when copper has started pinhole leaking, or when a house has had leak after leak in different places.

It is a planned project, not an emergency repair, but the first sign is often an emergency. If water is flowing where it should not be right now, start with burst pipe repair and come back to repiping once the water is off.

Signs that point to a repipe, not another patch

One leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks, or water that has changed, is something else. These are the signs that make us talk about repiping.

  • ✓Brown or rusty water when a tap has been idle. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, and the flakes show up as discolored water.
  • ✓Water pressure that has faded over the years. Rust and mineral buildup narrow old steel pipes until the opening is a fraction of the original.
  • ✓Repeated pinhole leaks. Tiny leaks in copper, especially at several places, can mean the pipe wall is thinning throughout, not just at one spot.
  • ✓Leaks in different places within a short time. Each repair relieves pressure and makes the next weakest spot fail.
  • ✓Stains on ceilings, floors or walls with no obvious source. Hidden leaks in walls often show up as stains first. Our leak detection service finds where the water is coming from.

Why older Guilford houses are candidates

The National Park Service nomination for Guilford's historic town center counts roughly 640 buildings in the district, with the largest group built after 1900 but a substantial share from earlier centuries. A house that has been renovated several times often has several generations of pipe in one building: original steel, mid-century copper and newer additions.

Mixed materials joined by brass fittings can corrode where metals meet. When we open a wall or ceiling to repair one leak, we look at what the neighboring pipe looks like. If the rest of the pipe is in the same state, we say so and show you.

Shoreline cottages that were later insulated and heated year-round bring another factor. Pipes that once ran in unheated space now sit behind finished walls, so a failure is hidden and costly.

Pipe materials: what we use and what to expect

The right material depends on the house, the building code and how much wall needs to be opened.

Which materials are allowed is set by the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). A licensed plumber builds to the code that applies and pulls any permit the job requires.

How a repipe is planned

Survey and quote

The plumber traces the existing lines, finds the water entry point, notes the fixtures and the wall and ceiling access, and tells you which walls would have to be opened.

Sequence and living at home

Whole-house repiping is usually done in stages so the house is without water for as short a time as possible. We discuss with you which bathrooms and kitchens lose water on which days.

Closing up

Pipes are pressure-tested before walls are closed. Drywall patching and painting are generally separate trades, so we tell you what the plumbing quote does and does not include.

What to do if a pipe fails while you are planning

A repipe is planned work, but old pipe does not wait for the plan. If one fails, do this.

Shut the main and open a low faucet

Closing the main and draining the line takes pressure off the break.

Do not tape or clamp over a corroded pipe

Corroded pipe next to a leak is usually thin. Pressure will find the next weak spot.

Keep furniture and electronics clear

Move what you can off the wet floor and out of the room below.

Call and mention the pipe type

Tell the dispatcher whether you see gray galvanized steel, copper or plastic.

What decides the price of a repipe

The price of repiping is not a single number for every house. These are the drivers.

  • Number of fixtures and floors.A two-bath ranch and a four-bath colonial are different jobs.
  • Access.Open basements and crawlspaces are quick. Plaster ceilings and tile walls are slow.
  • Material.PEX, copper and CPVC differ in material and labor.
  • Wall and finish repair.Drywall, plaster and paint are a separate cost.
  • Permit and inspection.Permit rules vary, so we ask the Guilford building department what your project needs.

Insurance usually pays for sudden water damage, not for replacing pipes that are simply old. If you have had a covered loss, ask your insurer whether any part of the repair applies. Call the 24/7 line for a plan and a clear price for your house.

Water entering the house: the service line

Repiping starts at the point where water enters the house. If your problem is low pressure that began right at the meter or wellhead, or water that comes in dirty, the issue may be outside the walls. The pipe from the street or well into the house is covered on our water service line page, which explains the difference.
