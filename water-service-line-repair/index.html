Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Guilford, CT

The water service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the street main or a private well to your house. When it breaks, pressure drops, water floods a yard or the meter spins with everything shut off. Our 24/7 line sends a plumber for emergency water service line repair in Guilford, CT.

This is different from the pipes inside the house. If a line inside is leaking, see leak detection. If the problem starts outside, read on.

Who owns what: utility, homeowner and well

On a public water system, the utility owns the main in the street. The pipe from the meter or curb valve to your house is generally the homeowner's to repair, and Connecticut Water can tell you where its responsibility ends at your address.

The public water in Guilford is supplied by Connecticut Water through what the company calls the Guilford System, which also serves Clinton, Madison, Old Saybrook and Westbrook, so questions about the meter or the main go to them.

A house on a private well has its own line from the wellhead or pressure tank. That line is entirely yours. A 2011 town-commissioned study found the Mulberry Point, Tuttles Point and Indian Cove waterfront communities of Guilford were then on individual wells, about 350 homes. If you are not sure which you have, look for a water meter and a utility bill.

Signs the service line has failed

Service line problems have a pattern.

  • ✓The meter moves with every fixture off. Water is leaving the system between the meter and the house or inside the house.
  • ✓Pressure drops through the whole house. A leak or restriction before the fixtures reduces pressure everywhere.
  • ✓A wet or sunken patch in the yard. Buried leaks come to the surface along the path of least resistance.
  • ✓Dirty or discolored water after work on the street. Disturbed sediment or a failing line.
  • ✓The sound of running water outside. At night, when everything is quiet, a leak can be audible near the meter box.

Low water pressure: where it starts

Low pressure has several causes, and not all are in the service line. A clogged filter, a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed main valve or corroded pipe inside the house can all cause it. A service line with a partial restriction or a slow leak is on the list.

The plumber checks pressure at an outdoor spigot and at the inside fixtures. If outside is strong and inside is weak, the problem is in the house piping and the repiping page applies. If both are weak, the service line or the supply is the suspect.

Lead and older materials

Connecticut Water's 2025 water quality report for the Guilford System says some older homes and buildings may have service lines made of lead or unknown materials, and it points customers to ctwater.com/serviceline to check. If your house is old, ask whether your line has been inspected. A replacement project is the time to remove any lead or unknown material.

Water service pipe materials and burial depth are governed by the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that the state adopts, with a 42 inch frost depth in the state code.

Digging, utilities and licensed work

Before an excavation with power equipment, the state requires a call to 811 or the Call Before You Dig website at least two full working days before digging. A contractor you hire must make the call. That keeps gas, electric and water utilities from being struck.

The state licenses plumbers who work on the line between the utility and a structure under a class called P-7, limited sewer, storm and water contractor, alongside the unlimited P-1 contractor license. You can check any license on the Connecticut license lookup. Permit rules vary, so ask the Guilford Building Department what your project needs.

What affects the price of service line work

Buried-pipe jobs are among the most variable in plumbing.

  • Length and depth.A short, shallow line is far less work than a long one under pavement.
  • What is on top.Lawn is cheap to restore. A driveway, patio or deck is not.
  • Method.Spot repair, trenchless and full replacement differ.
  • Materials.Copper and plastic pipe differ in cost and code requirements.
  • Permits and utility coordination.Utilities have their own requirements at the meter.

Insurance typically does not cover repair of a buried line from age or wear, though some policies offer an optional endorsement. Check yours.
