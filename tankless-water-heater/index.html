Tankless Water Heater Service in Guilford, CT

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it never runs out and takes far less space than a tank. It also asks more of your gas line, venting or electrical panel, and it needs periodic flushing to keep scale from choking the heat exchanger. We install, repair and descale tankless units in Guilford, CT.

Many tankless calls begin with the same sentence: it worked fine until it started throwing a code. This page explains what the codes tend to mean, what a flush does and when replacing the unit is the better call.

How a tankless unit differs from a tank heater

A tank heater keeps 40 to 80 gallons hot all day. A tankless unit fires a burner or electric elements the moment flow is detected and heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger. Flow rate, measured in gallons per minute, and the temperature rise from your incoming water decide whether one unit can run two showers and a dishwasher at once.

Incoming water on the Connecticut shoreline is coldest in winter, so a unit sized only for summer temperatures can disappoint in January.

When we quote a tankless installation we size it against the coldest water you will see and the most fixtures that run together.

The trade-off is infrastructure. A gas unit may need a larger gas line and its own vent. An electric unit can need heavy-gauge wiring and enough spare capacity in the electrical panel. A condensing gas model produces acidic condensate that must drain somewhere, often through a neutralizer.

Why flushing matters, and what a flush involves

Minerals in water come out of solution when heated and stick to the inside of the heat exchanger. Left alone, they insulate the metal, force the unit to work harder and cause overheating shutdowns. The signs that a flush is due are in the table above. The flush itself is a routine service.

  • 1Power and gas are shut off. The unit is isolated, and the cold and hot service valves are closed.
  • 2Hoses and a pump connect. A small pump circulates a descaling solution through the unit in a closed loop. Many homeowners try plain household vinegar, but manufacturers publish their own flushing procedures, and following the one in your manual protects the warranty.
  • 3The solution circulates for the time the manufacturer specifies. Then the system is rinsed with clean water.
  • 4The inlet filter is cleaned. A clogged filter is one of the simplest causes of low flow.
  • 5The unit is restarted and tested. We confirm flow, temperature and that no error code returns.

What scale means for a home on well water or public water

Hardness varies across the Guilford area depending on whether a home is on public water or a private well. We do not state a hardness figure for any address. Connecticut Water, which supplies public water in Guilford, says its annual water quality reports do not include hardness, and that its customer service can give an estimate for your area of the system.

The practical advice is the same either way: if you see white crust on faucets or kettles, schedule flushes more often and ask about a softener or scale reducer for the tankless unit.

When replacing the unit is smarter than repairing it

Tankless units are designed to last longer than tank heaters, but they are not maintenance-free. If a unit has never been flushed, has a cracked heat exchanger or has a circuit board failure beyond the warranty, repair cost can approach replacement cost.

We also look at the unit that is already there, and if it has failed in the dead of winter our emergency replacement service applies. A first-generation unit with parts that are hard to source is a poor candidate for another repair. If you are switching from a tank heater to tankless, or the reverse, the same questions about fuel, venting and gas capacity apply, and our water heater installation page covers the tank side.

For tankless units that have stopped heating altogether during a cold snap, the call is more urgent. Our water heater repair service covers emergency no-hot-water situations on both tank and tankless.

What decides the price of tankless work

Tankless work spans a quick flush to a full replacement with new venting. These items decide where yours lands, and the price is clear before work starts.

  • Flush or repair or replace.A flush is the smallest job. A control board or heat exchanger repair is larger. Full replacement depends on the model chosen.
  • Fuel type.Gas, propane and electric units have different installation requirements.
  • Venting.New vent routes through walls or the roof add materials and labor.
  • Gas line or electrical capacity.Upsizing a gas line or adding an electrical circuit can be a separate part of the quote.
  • Condensate and water treatment.A neutralizer or a scale reducer adds parts but protects the unit.

Warranty terms vary by manufacturer and often depend on installation by a licensed plumber and on documented maintenance. Keep your flush records. Want your real number? Call the 24/7 line.

Permits and who may do the work

Installing or replacing a water heater that burns gas or oil is regulated work in Connecticut. Permit rules vary, so ask the Guilford building department about what your project needs. A licensed plumber handles the permit as part of the job. The state adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and gas units follow NFPA 54 for natural gas and NFPA 58 for propane, so venting and gas supply are built to those standards.
