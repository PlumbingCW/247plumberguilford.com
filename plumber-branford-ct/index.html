Branford's houses are older than its neighbors'

Census data from the 2019 to 2023 American Community Survey puts the median Branford home at 1972, and about 46 percent of housing units were built before 1970. Of all housing units, 2,774, roughly one in five, date from 1939 or earlier. Homes of that age are where a plumber meets galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains and a bathroom that was added in 1955 and piped differently from the rest of the house.

The practical result is that Branford calls skew toward aging pipe: pinhole leaks in copper, rust-colored water, and drains that clog repeatedly because the line has bellied or the cast iron is scaled. If your house is from before 1960, say so on the call. It helps the plumber bring the right parts, and it often points to a repiping assessment after the emergency is over.

Emergency dispatch for Branford is available around the clock, which matters when a main line fails after the town offices have closed.

Municipal sewer in most of town, septic in some areas

The Town of Branford runs a Water Pollution Control Facility on Block Island Road, and its own page says the sewer system serves almost 85 percent of residences. The town's planning documents also identify some "sewer avoidance" areas where properties are expected to use on-site septic systems.

When a house is on the municipal sewer, a backup can come from the private lateral between the house and the town main, or from the main itself. A camera inspection tells which. If it is the lateral, the fix is the homeowner's. If it is the main, the town needs to know.

Our sewer camera inspection shows the pipe so the decision is not a guess. Branford is also part of the East Shore District Health Department, which handles septic system permits.

From Branford Center to Stony Creek

The town's plan of conservation and development names Branford Center as the key focal point, with villages at Stony Creek, Short Beach, Indian Neck and Pine Orchard, plus Branford Hills and Brushy Plains. Branford has four National Register historic districts, including Branford Center around the Town Green and the Stony Creek and Thimble Islands district.

Stony Creek in the southeast corner is a mention-only area for our line. Calls from Stony Creek and from neighboring East Haven are handled like any other Branford call. In older village houses, crawlspaces with pipes close to the ground are exposed to winter cold, so a freeze can start there.

Permits and licensed work in Branford

The Branford Building Department's page says a permit is required to install, alter, repair, remove, convert or replace an electrical, gas, mechanical or plumbing system that the code regulates. Its stated exemption is clearing or repairing leaks in plumbing systems that do not involve replacing or rearranging valves, pipes or fixtures. Permits are applied for online, and contractors must show Connecticut licensure and insurance.

For you, the practical point is that a straight leak repair is one thing and a replacement of a heater or a pipe run is another. Ask the plumber which side of that line your job falls on and who pulls the permit.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

Branford homes run from harbor-side cottages to inland ranches, and the first minutes of a plumbing emergency look alike in all of them.

Turn the main slowly

An older house may have a gate valve that sticks. Turn it steadily and do not force it, and tell the dispatcher if it will not move.

Check the neighbors for a backup

If sewage is coming up and the people next door have the same problem, say so. It tells the plumber whether to look at your lateral or the town main.

Move things off wet floors

Lift boxes and rugs, and keep anything electrical out of standing water.

Call and describe the house

Say about how old it is and whether the pipe near the main looks gray, copper or plastic.
