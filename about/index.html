How to check any plumber's Connecticut license

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbing work. Its plumbing classes include P-1, the unlimited plumbing contractor, and P-2, the unlimited journeyperson plumber, who may work only in the employ of a licensed contractor. Heating, piping and cooling work, including boilers, falls under separate S-series classes, and burner and gas work have their own B and G classes.

You can check a license on the state's license lookup. The Guilford Building Department also asks for a valid state license and liability insurance when a permit is applied for. That applies to us and to any contractor you hire.

What we do not do on this site

This site does not carry customer reviews, star ratings or case studies, and it does not list prices, because the work found at the property sets the price. It does not claim awards or memberships. If that changes, the page will say so with proof.

Where this site cites a fact about Guilford or a nearby town, it comes from the town's own documents, the state, the Census Bureau, NOAA, FEMA or a utility's published report. Where a number could not be confirmed from a primary source, we left it out.

Where the facts on this site come from

The local detail on these pages is drawn from public records. For Guilford that means the town's plan of conservation and development, its health, building and engineering departments, the Connecticut Water quality report for the Guilford System, FEMA flood maps, NOAA storm records and climate normals, and the Connecticut State Building Code. Housing figures for nearby towns come from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey.

We use those sources because a plumbing page should help you decide something. If a page mentions that Guilford has no municipal sewer, it is because the town's own plan says so, and because it changes what a plumber checks when a drain backs up.
