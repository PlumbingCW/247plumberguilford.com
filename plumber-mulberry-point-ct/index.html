The town's 2011 water supply study

Guilford commissioned a feasibility study for a water main. Its executive summary says the Mulberry Point, Tuttles Point and Indian Cove waterfront communities used individual wells and had experienced chronic drinking water quality and quantity problems. It covers about 350 homes and recommends connecting them to Connecticut Water.

We do not know whether the main was built, how far it went or whether your street is connected. That is the first thing to find out, because the answer decides which equipment your house depends on. Connecticut Water's customer line is 1-800-286-5700.

This is general plumbing information. Use it once you know which kind of supply you have.

If you receive a bill from Connecticut Water, you are on the public system.

Wells and septic tanks on the same small lot

Guilford's plan says septic effluent has contaminated nearby wells in some parts of town, and the town's septic brochure says sump pumps, footing drains and water softener backwash should be kept away from the septic system. It says nothing specific about Mulberry Point.

On a compact waterfront lot, a well, a tank and a field can sit close together. A plumber checks where the sump discharge and any softener backwash go, and whether the drains behave differently after heavy rain. See our sump pump service and drain cleaning page.

Questions to settle before a plumbing project here

Answer these first, and the quote you receive will fit the house.

  • ✓Are you on a well or on public water? The 2011 study is not proof of what is true today.
  • ✓When was the septic tank last pumped? The town requires pumping every five years.
  • ✓Has the well water been tested lately? Use a state-certified lab, and ask the Health Department at (203) 453-8118 which tests to request.
  • ✓Is any work inside a flood zone? The town requires a Flood Hazard Permit for some projects there.

If a water main reaches your street

Connecting a house to a new main usually means a new service line from the street to the building. Our water service line repair page describes that work. In Connecticut the contractor or the homeowner must call Call Before You Dig before power equipment goes in the ground, and the state's plumbing license classes include a limited license for water work from the utility to just inside a structure.

The old well and its pump do not disappear automatically. Ask the town health department what the rules are for a well that is taken out of service.
