Basement Waterproofing in Guilford, CT

A wet basement has one of several causes: groundwater pushing through the floor or walls, a plumbing leak that looks like seepage, condensation, or a drain that backs up. We help with the plumbing side of basement waterproofing in Guilford, CT, which starts with working out which one you have.

Some solutions belong to excavation and masonry contractors. We tell you where our work ends, so you do not pay for the wrong fix.

Rule out these causes before you buy a waterproofing system

A plumber can test several of these in one visit. Each one has a different fix.

  • 1A leaking supply or drain pipe. Water on the floor next to a wall may come from a pipe, not the ground. See leak detection.
  • 2Condensation on cold pipes and walls. Warm, humid air on cold surfaces drips and pools. Insulation and dehumidifying help.
  • 3A clogged or failing drain. A floor drain that backs up shows water that is not groundwater. See drain cleaning.
  • 4A failed sump pump. The pit fills, and the water has nowhere to go. See sump pump repair.
  • 5Surface drainage. Downspouts that end at the foundation and soil that slopes toward the house send water to the basement.
  • 6A water heater or washing machine leak. Appliances leak slowly and the water spreads across the floor.

Interior drains and sump pumps

An interior drain system sits along the inside of the footing under the slab and collects water before it reaches the floor. It drains to a sump pit where a pump sends it outside. This is the plumbing part of waterproofing, and a plumber can design the pit, the drain connections and the discharge.

The discharge matters in Guilford, because the town has no municipal sewer and its health department advises keeping sump pumps and footing drains away from the septic system. A system that dumps water onto the leach field can fail the septic system while saving the basement.

Why groundwater is a Guilford question

Guilford is a coastal town with tidal wetlands along its rivers and creeks, and its plan of conservation and development warns that rising water tables in coastal areas can make septic systems fail. NOAA's climate normals for 1991 to 2020 show about 42 inches of annual precipitation at the coastal New Haven Tweed station and about 50 inches at inland Mount Carmel. Wet years raise the water table, and a basement below the water table lets water in.

If your basement has taken water in a storm, ask whether the property is in a FEMA flood zone. Guilford's Engineering Department says work in the Special Flood Hazard Area can require a Flood Hazard Permit, and a plumber can describe which plumbing work is involved.

Paint, sealers and what they cannot do

Waterproofing paint and sealers coat the wall surface. They can help with minor dampness, but they do not stop water pushing through under pressure, and they can peel when water gets behind them. Treat them as a cosmetic layer, not a drainage system. If there is liquid water, it needs somewhere to go.

Where our work ends

We handle pumps, interior drains, drain lines, supply leaks and the plumbing connections of a waterproofing job. We do not excavate foundations or apply exterior membranes. When the cause is outside the wall, we say so, and you can take that finding to the contractor you choose, which saves you from paying for a plumbing fix that will not work.

What affects the price of the plumbing side of waterproofing

Costs depend on the cause.

  • The cause.A repaired pipe costs far less than an interior drain with a sump pump.
  • Length of interior drain.More perimeter means more concrete to cut and patch.
  • Pump size and backup.Higher flow and battery backups add equipment.
  • Discharge route.A long run and a septic-safe outlet add work.
  • Finished space.Drywall and flooring must be moved or replaced.
