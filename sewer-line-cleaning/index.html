Sewer backup: what is happening in the pipe

The main line carries all the waste from the house to the sewer main or the septic tank. When it blocks, the lowest fixtures show it first: the basement floor drain, a first-floor shower, the laundry standpipe. Water backs up there because it has nowhere else to go.

The causes are familiar. Grease and wipes that were flushed collect at bends. Roots enter through joints in older pipe and form a mat that catches everything. A section that has sagged, called a belly, holds water and waste. Sometimes the pipe has collapsed. Our sewer line repair page explains when cleaning is not enough.

The service is similar, but the questions and the next steps are not. In Guilford the right-hand column applies, because the town has no municipal sewer.

Sewage backup: the safe steps

Sewage carries bacteria, so treat it with care.

  • 1Stop all water use. Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher.
  • 2Keep people and pets out of the wet area. Children and pets should stay out until the area is cleaned.
  • 3Turn off power if water reaches outlets or appliances. Only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry surface.
  • 4Do not use the basement bath or floor drain. They are the backup points.
  • 5Call the 24/7 line and photograph the damage. Photos help with an insurance claim.

Guilford runs on septic systems, and the town sets the rules

Guilford's 2015 plan of conservation and development says the town does not have sewers, and the Guilford health department says sewage disposal is handled on-site by individual septic systems and community systems. Its page also says every septic system must be pumped every five years and that, based on lot size and soil suitability, several areas of town must be inspected yearly.

The health department asks pumpers to return a record of each cleaning, and it says septic repairs need a health department permit and a licensed septic contractor. That is the dividing line for us. A plumber clears and repairs the pipes in the house and the line to the tank. The tank, the leach field and any pumping belong to septic professionals working under that permit.

If the tank is the problem, a plumber can confirm that the house line is clear and say when to call a pumper. See our location pages for Madison and Clinton for how those towns handle it.

Cleaning methods for the main line

A drain cable machine with a root cutter head clears most main line blockages. For heavy buildup, hydro jetting flushes the pipe wall after the cable has opened a path. A camera confirms the pipe is clear and shows whether it has damage. Cleaning without a camera is a guess when the line is old or the problem keeps returning.

If your house has no cleanout, the plumber may install one as part of the visit. A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line, and it makes every later visit cheaper and faster.

What affects the price of sewer line cleaning

A clear price comes before work starts. These factors move it.

  • Access to the line.With a cleanout, setup is quick. Without one, the plumber works through a toilet or roof vent.
  • Length and depth.A main that runs sixty feet to the street is more involved than a short run.
  • Cause of the blockage.Roots, grease and a collapsed pipe need different tools.
  • Camera inspection.Often added to confirm results.
  • When you call.After-hours visits are quoted as emergencies.

Sewer backup coverage is often a separate endorsement on a homeowners policy. Check yours and keep photos. A pumper's invoice is separate from the plumber's.
